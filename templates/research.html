Interpretation of Socioeconomic Factors vs Diabetes (Balanced)

  • Income Levels: Lower income brackets (<$10k to $20–25k) show a higher proportion of diabetes. For example, the <$10k group has 115 diabetes cases out of 320 respondents (36%), whereas the ≥ $75k group has 1396 cases out of 4226 respondents (33%). This suggests limited resources and access can drive higher diabetes risk.
  • Education Levels: Lower education categories (None/K to Grades 9–11) exhibit dramatically higher diabetes proportions. E.g., None/K: 14 cases of 19 total (74%), Grades 1–8: 119 of 234 (51%), compared to College grads: 1366 of 4428 (31%). This indicates higher education correlates with lower diabetes prevalence, likely via better health literacy and behaviors.

Balanced classes reveal that socioeconomic factors—both income and education—play a significant role in diabetes prevalence, with lower SES linked to higher risk.
